# Preferences going in

The user's picks from past treatment rounds, so the first direction set and the make start from his taste instead of the average. Try these first; put the alternative in the set only when the brief gives a reason. Update at every skill pause.

- Abstract, data-drawn treatments over illustration and collage. A visual generated from the product's own data beats cartoon, big type, and a wallpaper. "The visualization is slick" is the reaction to aim for.
- Abstract plus proof. The abstract visual carries the idea; a strip of real examples carries the credibility. He combined them himself.
- Placement follows the job: the visual in the lower third with the form above, the strip along the bottom edge. Centered, louder, quieter, and minimal variants of a placed visual all lost to the placed one.
- One accent, hard occlusion, one focal element. Depth by three pre-blended grays, light background lines (0.6 at 1x, his pick over the scorecard's 1px floor), the focal element at three times their weight with air around it.
- No exact copy on the visual, no label explaining the strip. The story lives in the mark.
- Strips and cards lead with the number, then the name, then one comma-separated meta line. Chunky cards with a number-first slant; the strip fits its frame with air below.
- Empty states are glyphs from the product's domain. The domain's own objects for the product-specific moments (no results, first run); generic states like a load error can use a generic glyph. Centered as a block with the sentence and one link. A miniature of the big visual under a no-results line meant nothing to him.
- Vector only so far; raster hasn't been asked for. A product's chosen visual is its decorative vocabulary; later surfaces reuse it before opening a new axis.
- First-run empty states keep the frame: the toolbar stays, three dashed card slots at the real card size, the sentence and the one primary action inside the first slot with a small sourced glyph (48px) above them. Ghost slots beat a centered glyph on a 140px stage, a backdrop, and text only.
- Generic objects are sourced, not drawn: if it's something generic, it doesn't need to be custom, and that's the first check. Custom stays for the product-specific glyph, and custom has to be constructed, rendered, and checked, never sketched.
- A backdrop is fixed. It never shifts, scales, or re-seeds to make room for what sits on top; the foreground group occludes it (a paper background on the form group, or the generator's guard zone sized to the tallest state).
