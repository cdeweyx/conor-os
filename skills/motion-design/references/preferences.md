# Preferences

The user's picks from past runs. Start the frame from these; add to them at the skill pause.

- The wait on the core computation is the moment that matters most. It's "a magic moment" that should feel rich, interactive, and wow; it outranks every other motion on the product.
- A front-door backdrop draws in once and then sits still. The wow budget goes to the computation, not the homepage.
- Small enter motions on secondary elements (a warning callout appearing, an empty-state glyph drawing) aren't worth prototyping; cut them unless they carry the product's claim.
- Critics before the user. The user sees a version that has already survived two or three scored rounds, and the loop may reopen the sequence.
- Be generative first. The user expects a wide candidate list and shouldn't have to nudge for the big one.
- A line draw-in holds 400ms on the finished line; labels appear as the line reaches what they name; the live heading counts the number the page lands on.
- Copy on a moving chart: as little as possible ("dunno if we need any copy there"); name what the marks are, never borrow a number from another concept.
- A success state holds long enough to land before the surface goes away, and a follow-on action offered after it stays long enough to reach. A confirmation popup went from closing 350 ms after its success state to 600 ms ("give a quick beat to enjoy the saved state, like the tiniest amount more time"), and the window for its follow-on action from 1.5 s to 3 s ("too quick ... double whatever it is").
- Cost-bearing actions never fire from browsing.
- "I trust you" is trust to execute, not to redesign the pick.
- Explain every call in plain words: what the user sees in each option, then the lean.

- A marketing landing page is richer than the app: the whole page drops and settles in on load with the backdrop drawing, each section animates as it scrolls in and feels "fluid, nice, and alive", and the nav's hover should "feel really rich and nice". The app's quiet rules (a hover never moves anything, nothing on first render) stay in the app. "A front-door backdrop draws in once and then sits still" still holds for the backdrop itself.
- Clicks and small state changes: "I'll trust you there." Spend the asks on load, scroll and the signature moments.
- Don't draw motion out on Paper. Paper holds the states sheets (hover, focus, pressed, selected, busy are static) and start, peak and settled frames for each signature moment; timing pairs and directions go in one prototype with toggles. A 140-artboard strip round was cut to about 20.
- Fixing critic findings isn't enough; the user wants the work pushed up, not just cleaned. After a first round scored 5 to 7, "Be more ambitious. Get that taste up." Each critic round gets a planner pass that raises every moment toward a 9 with bolder choreography, not only the fix list.
- A gliding nav pill is the liked direction, but it must stay a soft tint: hovering one link never dims or blacks out its siblings, and the pill never overshoots ("the current state's wonky ... everything else gets blacked out"). Opening beats of a staged wait get checked live for flashes and jumps; Conor felt "buggy" there that no critic caught from sheets.
- On a landing page, sections settling in from half opacity as they scroll in is liked, even though critics read it as a generic fade-up. Don't cut it on a critic's say-so.
- A light motion pass on a flow whose stills he already approved: one prototype of the signature moment and its handoff, the rest written as fades on existing tokens in the brief. He leaves the motion calls to the lead.
