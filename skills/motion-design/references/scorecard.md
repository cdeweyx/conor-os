# Scorecard for motion

The taste critic scores a contact sheet and stills on each line, yes or no, then gives a 0 to 10. Anything still failing after three rounds is a direction problem, not a polish problem.

1. One job. A viewer can say what the motion is for in five words.
2. Real, not theater. Every moving mark stands for data or a user action. Nothing loops to fill time; no fake progress.
3. Order is the story. What happened first moves first; cause before effect, work before verdict.
4. One focal point at a time. In every still, the eye knows where to go.
5. A landing beat. There's a clear moment it stops, and the result reads within 200ms of it.
6. Stillness after. Nothing keeps moving that doesn't need to; loops are slow, linear, and pausable.
7. Timing in range. Durations and easings are in the rulebook's range or a named exception; exits faster than entrances; linear only when data sets the pace.
8. Physical. Things grow from where they start; paired elements share easing and duration; nothing animates layout.
9. Respects the user. Skippable and interruptible, short on a repeat, never blocks typing, reduced motion follows rulebook rule 7.
10. None of the tells. No fade-up stagger, shimmer, a spinner where the work could show, bounce on opacity, glow, confetti, or a progress bar that isn't measuring anything.

11. Serves the brief. For a signature moment, it delivers what the brief asks the viewer to feel (rich, wow, a thing worth watching twice). Restraint that loses the brief is a fail, not a pass.

Bonus, for a signature moment: it couldn't belong to another product, and a user would describe it to someone else.
