# Rubric and critic protocol

What the blind critics score. Built from D&AD's three questions (a brilliant idea, great execution, answered the brief), Haviv's appropriate-distinctive-simple, Neumeier's swap, hand, and field tests, Bierut's "how it is used", and Ehrenberg-Bass on distinctiveness (groups judge it better than individuals, which is why it's the median of three).

## What the critics see

The final brand board, three branded product screens (the hardest ones, not the prettiest), a copy sample (five real product lines rewritten in the brand's voice: an empty state, an error, a confirmation, a heading, a button), and the line-up sheet. No brief, no code, no prior scores, no direction names.

**Done before:** the list from `preferences.md`, given to every critic here and in the pre-show passes. The critic flags any move the work reuses from another product's brand; the lead checks each flag against the direction's "Defaults kept" reasons, and a reuse with no reason is a fix.

**Line-up sheet:** six marks at the same size, names hidden, shuffled: ours, four competitors' marks, and one decoy generated by a plain model prompt for the same category. Below it, the six wordmarks set the same way.

## Axes (0 to 10 each)

- **Idea and fit, 20%.** Field test: can the critic say in one sentence what this brand is and who it's for? Does the idea come from the product's subject rather than the category's clichés? Hand test on the copy sample: cover the mark, can you still tell who's talking?
- **Distinctiveness, 25%.** On the line-up, does the critic pick ours as the product, and not as the generated one? Would any two marks be better swapped (Neumeier's swap test)? At least two assets beyond color (a shape, a type treatment, a device) that could be shown unbranded and still be named.
- **Mark craft, 15%.** Reads at 16px, in one color, and under a squint. Could be doodled from memory. No cliché shape unless the idea demands it and the critic can say why.
- **System in the product, 20%.** The product looks like the board, not the board stuck on a stock app: type, neutrals, numerals, radii, chart tone, empty states. Data screens still read as data.
- **Tells, 20%.** Start at 10, lose 2 per tell from `tells.md`, floor 0.

## Caps (override the arithmetic)

- Ours is picked as the generated decoy, or a swap with a competitor improves both: overall 6 at most.
- The mark fails at 16px or in one color: mark craft 5 at most, overall 6.5 at most.
- Three or more tells: overall 6 at most.
- Product screens still show the pre-brand defaults: system 5 at most.
- The palette is one of the named clusters in `tells.md`: distinctiveness 6 at most.

## Answer format

In this order: what it saw in two seconds; which line-up mark it thinks is generated and which belongs to this product, with why; the idea in one sentence if it can find one; the five scores with caps applied; the top three fixes, each with the screenshot it points at, what to change, the effect expected, and the cost. "Improve contrast" isn't a fix; "text-3 on the background to 4.6:1 for the axis labels" is. Prefer removing to adding.

## Running it

Three critics, median per axis, then the weighted overall with caps applied; that overall is the round's score. Fix the top three findings between rounds. Three rounds at most; stop at an overall 9 or when a round gains under a point. Every round's prompt lists the user's settled calls ("the accent is the user's pick; don't re-argue it"). Log scores, the line-up picks, findings, and what changed to `mockups/brand/critique.md`. A finding that contradicts a call the user made loses; log it with the call it lost to.

## The pre-show passes (directions)

Both direction rounds get one before the user sees them. Three `critic` agents (Model: critic role), work shuffled and unlabeled, one round, no fixes.

- **Round 1, the cards.** Each critic gets the ten to twelve cards, each beside its reference. It scores each card on idea and fit, distinctiveness, and craft against its reference (is it as good as what it borrowed from, or a weaker copy?), names the three it would develop, and flags any two cards that read as the same direction. A pair flagged by two critics gets one card replaced before showing.
- **Round 2, the full boards.** Each critic gets the boards (with their real screens) plus a line-up of their marks (one per board, the agent's pick from its sketches) beside four competitors' and one decoy. It scores idea and fit, distinctiveness, tells, and craft against the references, and says which board it would pick and why.

The medians go next to the agent's own scoring table in `directions.md`; the scales differ, so compare the order, and where the two orders disagree, say so in the recommendation.
