# Preferences

The user's picks from past brand runs: what was chosen over what, and why. Read before the kickoff work; a pick here is the starting point, not a question to ask again. 

## Brand idea

- **On the nose beats clever.** A clever line loses when it "doesn't mean anything at a glance". Lead with plain, literal lines about what the product does.

## Borrowed world

- Pick a borrowed world with color and personality (TV broadcast graphics, a weather forecast, a graded exam in Sharpie) over a quiet record-keeping object ("boring") or dense newsprint. Offer many to react to, then weave the picks into blends.

## References

- Drawn to physical objects living in the UI (a paper card clipped inside a UI, a product as a postage stamp, a holographic membership card) and print texture (a halftone dot chart); cut anything neon-glow or liquid-glass.

## Directions

- **Range first, and craft from references.** Three territories (all light grounds, one shared scheme, built as CSS overrides on the live app) were "not nearly broad enough"; the user wanted "way more exploration", the word "directions" instead of "territories", and higher craft by leaning "heavier on examples and inspo vs. creating from the ground up." Hence the wide round of reference-led cards with spread floors.
- **Unique but intuitive.** The winning card had the "colors working the best". The category's own colors alone are "too generic". An element from a cut card can carry over (a card's order of content, the texture of one chart) without its look or layout.
- **A chart axis stays literal** (time on x, the value on y), even when a borrowed object offers a cleverer axis.
- **A display face goes on titles only**, over the app's text face; an all-display and an all-condensed system both lost.
- **Both color modes get settled at once**, on a black or white ground.
- **The icon set matches the type family** (the family's companion icon set, when it has one).
- **A reveal builds up to its payoff** ("the ending was given away" when everything shows first), and it's snappy.
- **Brand copy uses no semicolons**, and the category's buzzwords are out.
- **An event the product celebrates stands out by dimming everything else**, not with a new hue or a filled chip ("white background looks bad"). Short copy over complete copy ("Due on 1 invoice", not "...this week"). A count in the nav reads better bare than boxed. Product concepts that leak into the UI without a user meaning come out.

## Done before

Motifs, objects, and moves used in past brands, one line each, dated, with the product. A round 1 card that reuses one says so on the card; a round 2 board that keeps it writes the reason in its "Defaults kept" field. The user liking a world counts as a reason once it's written down. Critics get this list. Add each run's lines at the skill pause.
