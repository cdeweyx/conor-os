# Anatomy by visitor job

What a page is built around when the product isn't a software signup, or isn't built yet (a probe). Read the one job that fits at the argument step, before writing claims: it sets where the action sits, which sections are the category's own, and how the page ends. The argument still decides what each section claims; this decides what shape the claims take. Adapted from Hallmark's anatomy (github.com/Nutlope/hallmark, MIT), which drew it from about fifty independent homepages. Text beside a picture, a figure row, three steps, and an email band is how software pages look and almost nothing else.

Every job: the nav carries the job's own verb (Book, Tickets, Shop, Listen, Start free), the hero shows the thing itself (the room, the cover, the dates, the screen), and proof comes in the category's own form, never a generic band of numbers. When a section's content wasn't given, build a sample the user replaces (an example issue, dishes typical of the cuisine, track titles as placeholders), named in the handoff. A sample shows the shape of the real thing and never states a fact a visitor could take as true; a missing fact gets a slot (`polish.md`, honest gaps).

Two more hold for every job. An action the visitor types (an email, a prompt, a search) sits open on the page with its button, never behind a click, a modal, or a reveal. A product that reads the visitor's private data (inbox, messages, files, screen) treats trust as a section, since it's the first objection: what it reads, where it runs, what it keeps and for how long, a Security link in the nav, and the strongest fact in the first screen's proof line (on-device, open source, never trained on, deleted after a stated time; a `[NEED: ...]` slot when none is confirmed yet).

## subscribe (a newsletter, a waitlist, a podcast)

- **Action:** in the hero, and often it is the hero: the name, one line, the email field. A podcast puts the latest episode and its play control under it.
- **Sections:** the latest issue set as it arrives; who writes it; the archive as dated rows; the reader count only if given.
- **Ends:** a second field under the archive. The one job whose close is naturally an email field.

## book (a restaurant, a class, a clinic, a studio)

- **Action:** a booking picker (party, date, time) from the nav and the hero, or the next free times as buttons; repeated beside the menu or timetable, never only at the foot.
- **Sections:** the room or the food as a photograph with little or no heading; the menu with prices; for classes, the timetable; how to find it; hours and address as their own section.
- **Ends:** hours, address, and the booking again. A fine room can be almost nothing: one photograph, the menu, the booking.

## visit (a shop, a gallery, a venue, a place)

- **Action:** today's hours and the address in the nav or the hero; tickets only if they exist.
- **Sections:** what's on now as the lead picture; what's coming as dated rows; for a shop, the shelves; getting there.
- **Ends:** visit information, or the list for what's on next.

## attend (an event, a festival, a conference)

- **Action:** tickets in the nav from the first screen; the date and place are the headline.
- **Sections:** the lineup or speakers with names set large; the days as a schedule; ticket tiers with prices; venue and travel; questions.
- **Ends:** tickets again or the questions. Between editions the whole page is the date, the place, and the list.

## buy (a physical product, a small shop)

- **Action:** the product with its price and options, often as the hero; a buy action after the sections that earn it, or a sticky bar with the price.
- **Sections:** the range with prices; the product in use, close; how it's made; delivery and the guarantee as plain facts; reviews only if real.
- **Ends:** the offer again or the guarantee. An email field belongs in the footer, not as the close.

## try (an app, a game, a desktop tool)

- **Action:** store or download buttons right under the headline, one per real platform.
- **Sections:** the key art, trailer, or main screen from the product (`product-ui.md`); features as named moments shown in screens, not icon tiles; a before and after where the product changes something; platforms.
- **Ends:** the download again beside one last screen. Games are short: five or six blocks is a full page.

## sign up (software, an API, a developer tool)

The default the steps assume. Start free in the nav and hero with its terms in one line under it (for a developer tool the install command can be the action); the product at work; named moments; a code sample or the integration; pricing with its period switch; questions. Ends on the hero's action in a short band or the code sample, never a newsletter.

## probe (asking for money before the build)

A page for a product that doesn't exist yet, there to find out whether anyone pays. A pre-order to a warm audience is the only validation with a hard conversion number (one founder's pre-order page took money from 16% of 10,351 visitors in a day), and waitlists convert weakly (one case: about 3% at launch); both founder-reported. A waitlist asked for as a demand test is this job: say so and offer the price.

- **Action:** a real price and a pre-order or paid-pilot button that takes the money (a payment link is enough), in the hero and at the close. Never an email field: a signup costs nothing, so it proves nothing.
- **Sections:** the offer as the hero (who it's for, what they get, the price, when it ships); the problem in the buyer's words from MESSAGE.md; what they'd get, shown as a labeled sample or sketch, never a screen claiming it works today; the terms (the ship date, the refund if it doesn't); who's making it. There's no product to operate, so the live-thing floor doesn't apply.
- **Ends:** the price and the button again, with the refund line.
- **Judged:** the bar is written before posting (a number of paid pre-orders; three is a fair start for a small product), then the page is posted where the buyer already gathers (MESSAGE.md's sources say where), then read at about day 7 on money taken and specific buying feedback (a question about the price, the one feature they'd need), not visits or signups.

## hire (a studio, a freelancer, an architect)

- **Action:** a plain contact (an email, a phone, "Talk to us") in the nav and once more after the work; no form needed.
- **Sections:** the work is the page: projects listed with name, client, year, and pictures, one opened as a case; what the studio does as a short list; where it is.
- **Ends:** contact details, per office if there are several.

## join (a club, a community, a membership)

- **Action:** join, or "your first visit", in the hero.
- **Sections:** what a session is like, shown; when and where; membership options as rows with prices; first-visit guidance; people only in the user's own pictures.
- **Ends:** the first visit or the membership options.

## listen (a band, a film, a publication)

- **Action:** the new release or current issue with its listen, watch, or order buttons side by side.
- **Sections:** the release (cover, tracks or contents); dates as rows with ticket links; earlier work as covers; videos.
- **Ends:** the dates or more of the catalog. With no release, the act leads and the page's job is booking them, so the close is the enquiry.

## How pages end

In the order real sites use them: the hero's action again in a short band; practical details (hours, address, contacts); more of the catalog; a short trust tail (a guarantee, press the user gave); a mailing list, only for things that recur (issues, editions, releases, events). The list is about one close in five, never the default.
