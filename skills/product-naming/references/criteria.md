# Criteria, tests, and the lint

How names get cut and scored. Sources: Catchword's naming guide (types, ten criteria, two-phase generation), Igor (four categories, the competitor map), Lexicon via Lenny's Newsletter (decoupled teams, the competitor test, sound symbolism), Neumeier (seven criteria), Alexandra Watkins (SMILE and SCRATCH), glacierphonk/naming via cofoundy (metaphor questions, swap and compound tests, prior art first).

## Name types

Sort the competitors' names into these first; the empty corner is where to look.

| Type | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | Hotels.com | Says what it does. Weak to protect, easy to forget, where most category names cluster. Professionals steer away. |
| Suggestive | Fitbit, Robinhood | Hints at the benefit. The most common strong type. |
| Evocative | Apple, Linear | Evokes the positioning, not the function. Must match the brand idea or it's noise. |
| Real word, re-meant | Slack, Stripe, Notion | Easier to remember than coined words; hardest to find free. |
| Coined, root visible | Figma, Vercel | Distinctive and ownable while the root still shows. |
| Coined, no root | Syncora, Nexagen | Hard to remember, reads as generated. Avoid. |

## The tests (pass or cut)

- **Swap test.** Put the name on three products in unrelated categories. If it works on all three, it's generic. (Lantern works for a notes app, a VPN, and a candle shop: cut.)
- **Compound test.** For a two-part name, cover each half. Does the other half still say something specific? Smart, Auto, Cloud, Hub, Flow, Forge halves fail.
- **Drawable.** Can you sketch the name as a picture? A name with an image gives the mark something to work with.
- **Origin story.** Can you say why it fits in 15 seconds, from the product's mechanism? No story, no name.
- **Competitor test.** "A competitor just launched with this name; how do you feel?" Envy is a good sign.
- **Radio test.** Heard once, can someone spell it? Said once, can they pronounce it?

## SCRATCH (the kill screen)

Any one is a cut: Spelling-challenged, Copycat (sounds like a competitor), Restrictive (boxes in the product's future), Annoying (forced puns, cute misspellings), Tame (flat, safe, descriptive), Curse of knowledge (only insiders get it), Hard to pronounce.

## Scorecard (1 to 5 each, top 15 to 20 go to the checks)

| Criterion | What scores high |
|---|---|
| Fits the brand idea | You could explain the idea from the name |
| Distinctiveness | Nobody in the category sounds like it; it sits in the empty corner of the type map |
| Brevity | Two syllables or fewer is ideal; three is fine; four needs a reason |
| Easy to say and spell | Passes the radio test cold |
| Likability | Pleasant to say out loud; a little polarizing is fine |
| Extendability | Survives the product growing (a second market, a team plan) |
| Protectability | Not a plain descriptor; clean on the first prior-art look |

Sound notes are flavor, not score: Lexicon finds V reads vibrant, B reliable, and repeated sounds sticky (Sonos, KitKat).

## The lint (run last, as a check)

Flag, then justify or cut. A blocklist is never the method; it moves the default.
- **2015 tells:** -ly, -ify, -io, -ai, -hub, -flow, -base, dropped vowels (Flickr-style), AI tacked on.
- **Model favorites:** Nova, Nexus, Flux, Echo, Pulse, Forge, Sage, Apex, Vertex, Zenith.
- **2026 tells:** cozy artisan nouns (Lantern, Ember, Cairn, Harbor, Compass, Lighthouse, Acorn, Kettle, Pebble), and the category pun (Quill or Scribe for writing, Ledger or Tally for accounting).
- **Category clichés for this run:** the three named in brand-design's brand idea step (or in the brief, when this skill runs alone).
